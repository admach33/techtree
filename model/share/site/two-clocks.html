The proof is ready.The rocket isn’t.

AI labs now produce research-grade mathematics on request, for about $200 to $2,000 of compute a result before checking (The Decoder). None of the results in OpenAI's October 2026 mathematics release has been independently verified yet: the proofs are agent-written and unchecked by outsiders, the lowest of our trust tiers. Outside teams get the capability about 2027 in our central scenario. We traced what that does to space technology through 2035, across 478 milestones in 13 branches. It comes down to two clocks.

Thinking is about a third of a hardware program's critical path. In the central scenario it runs 5 to 10 times faster while fabrication, test and qualification gain 10 to 20%, which caps a new program at about 1.6× faster by 2031 (1.5 to 1.7× across those inputs). That is the ceiling; realized gains are smaller, about a third shorter by 2035 for programs finished by then.

The bottleneck

164 outcomes. Thinking is the bottleneck for none of them.

For every system in service and strategic outcome, we assign a binding gate: the gate whose removal would move its earliest year the most in the central scenario. A smarter AI pulls these outcomes forward 1.2 to 1.7 years on average from Plateau to Exponential, and it decides how many arrive at all: of hardware milestones are unlocked by 2035 across the four scenarios. But thinking is never the slowest step. It is the runner-up gate for : remove the binding gate and the math or design would hold the date next. That is how thinking can be a third of a program's critical path and bind no outcome: a third of the path can run fast without ever being the slowest step. As thinking gets cheap, the bottleneck moves: first to specifying, checking, data and institutions, and by the mid-2030s to physics and paperwork.

One dot per operational system or strategic outcome, grouped by the gate we assign as binding. Launch splits in two: SpaceX's own manifest (Starlink, its orbital compute, lunar tankers and cargo, Mars) and outside customers, who wait for capacity.

The bridge

Robots are the bridge between the clocks. They arrive late.

Robots are the one thing in the model that acts on the hardware clock itself; without them, AI trims factories, tests and paperwork 1.1 to 1.5×. By 2035, with robots, the ceiling on a new program rises to 2.3 to 3.7×. In the Embodied case the fleet grows fast, but it stays a small share of the work for years, and outside the robots branch it moves no launch, landing, regulator or budget date.

For investors

Capital can buy down some gates. Others run on their own clock.

We read the bottleneck chart as a map of where money moves dates and where it waits.

Capital can buy down

  • Capital itself, the single largest gate: funded programs, factories and fleets.
  • Some fabrication: second production lines, array and radiator plants, stacks.
  • Some qualification: more test stands and parallel test articles, though each data point on engine life still costs a flight.

Capital cannot buy down

  • Launch cadence: pads, propellant and environmental caps, and for outside customers SpaceX's decision to release capacity.
  • Regulators: site reviews, spectrum and certification rules.
  • Adoption: operators choosing to trust AI-made analysis and new service models.

Who sits on the hardware clock

  • SpaceX: Starship cadence sets refueling, AI1 orbital compute, lunar cargo, Mars and direct-to-cell.
  • Outside launch customers: compute operators such as Starcloud, small lunar missions, other constellations.
  • Competing launchers: Blue Origin, Rocket Lab and Chinese reusable fleets, which shape when SpaceX sells spare capacity.

What to watch, 2027 to 2029

  • Starship ship reflights in 2027, and 100 heavy flights a year about 2030.
  • An outside team checking OpenAI's results, and the capability reaching outside engineering teams in 2027.
  • An evidence rule for AI-made engineering analysis, about 2029 (Medium).
  • Starship spare capacity offered to customers: 2031 in Mach33's reference case, tested 2029 to 2033.

Mars windows still open every 26 months, whatever the AI.

Mars cargo can leave only in those windows (2031, 2033 and 2035 within our horizon), so a missed window costs two years whatever the analysis says. Space has its own exponential, and it is physical: reusable heavy lift taking launch cost down for reasons that owe nothing to AI mathematics. Cheap launch reaches SpaceX's own programs first. Outside customers wait for Starship capacity, about 2031 in Mach33's Launch Keystone v1.3, and new rideshare bookings beyond early 2029 were already closed by June 2026. Our closing claim: capital and adoption gate the most outcomes, launch gates the space-specific ones, and a rule for what AI-made analysis is worth as evidence is the lever policy can pull. That rule is possible from about 2029 in the central scenario, rated Medium (30 to 70%) from 2030 and still Medium by 2035.

How to read this

Each milestone has an earliest credible year and odds for every year to 2035: High (over 70%), Medium (30 to 70%), Low (10 to 30%), Speculative (under 10%). We judge the odds as analysts; we do not compute them. The year marks when a milestone first becomes credible, and the odds carry the expectation. Robot milestones are dated on the base robot path, but their odds also include a faster robot rollout.

Scenarios

Plateau: AI task horizons double every 12 to 18 months. Exponential (our central scenario): every 6 to 9 months. Recursive: AI speeds up its own research from 2028. Embodied: Recursive plus general-purpose robots at scale.
