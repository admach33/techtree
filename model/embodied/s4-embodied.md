# S4 Embodied: when the cognitive exponential gets hands

**Final analysis for the Space Unlock Tree · 7 October 2026 · data in [s4-embodied.json](s4-embodied.json) · draft reviewed by a physical-constraints skeptic and an acceleration advocate**

## 1. The answer in brief

The question: if OpenAI's Oct 6 math release is one of three, does the acceleration reach the physical world through robots, and do robots then speed up every physical domain? We answer with a fourth scenario, **S4 Embodied**: S3 Recursive cognition plus general-purpose robots at scale, including robots that help build robots.

- **Robotics is bleeding in. It is fast in rate and small in share.** In the base path the general-purpose robot fleet reaches about 6 million in 2031 and about 60 million in 2035. That is 0.7% of global manufacturing labor in 2031 and about 9% in 2035.
- **"Quickly" means the 2030s.** Robots become useful for tolerance-critical aerospace work at leading firms around 2030-31. Most physical milestones in the tree land in 2028-31, before that.
- **Robots raise throughput far more than they cut latency.** More robots let more programs and experiments run at once. A single program gets shorter only where its time is labor or queue. Thermal-vacuum dwells, creep tests, hot-fire failures, reliability statistics, Mars windows, trial endpoints and statutory reviews do not shrink.
- **Amdahl, with physics held fixed.** A new space program's compression ceiling goes from 51% (S3) to 52% for a typical program in 2031, 56-60% at a robot-first firm, and 66-67% at a robot-first firm in 2035. Infinite cognition plus infinite robots stops at about 67-73%.
- **"Every domain" moves at the pace of its slowest essential input.** By 2035 robot production, lab experiments and integrated space hardware can double output in under two years. Grid power, construction and drug approvals cannot.
- **In the tree, S4 changes almost nothing by 2031.** No tier 5-7 node moves by a year or a band. Six gain about a quarter-year. The year-scale gains arrive in 2032-35.
- **The rumor is unconfirmed.** If true, it is evidence about the cognitive clock, not about robots or launch dates.

## 2. What the rumor would and would not imply

No OpenAI statement or named report calls the Oct 6 release one of three dumps. [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) has a single commit, and OpenAI wrote only of "future releases" ([Engadget, Oct 7 2026](https://www.engadget.com/2279815/openai-just-posted-hundreds-more-results-on-major-math-problems/)). The rumor likely blends three OpenAI math releases in 9.5 weeks (Aug 1, Sep 8, Oct 6), staff remarks about held-back results ([Scientific American, Oct 6](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-unleashes-hundreds-more-math-results-upon-a-field-already-in-shock/)) and reported progress on other Millennium problems ([Fortune, Oct 7](https://fortune.com/2026/10/07/openai-math-controversy-solutions-370-outstanding-challenges-published-criticisms-celebration/)).

**If true, it implies:**

- A large backlog and fast posing and filtering (about 4,000 problems posed, 9% kept). Release dates measure disclosure policy, not throughput, so we compute no doubling time from them.
- Moderate evidence for upper S2 or an earlier S3: shift 10-15 points toward S3. The real test is whether each release comes from a newer, stronger model.

**It does not imply:**

- Faster robots. AI mathematics is worth about 1.1-1.5x on the robotics timeline, mainly through contact simulation and safety certificates. Robots are bound by data, hands, field hours, actuators and certification.
- Shorter space programs. Infinite cognition moves the ceiling only from 51% to 53%.
- Much earlier S4. An early-onset case (useful robots from 2027) lifts the 2031 base fleet from 6 to 9 million but adds under 1 point to a typical 2031 program.

## 3. How fast AI reaches the physical world through robots

**Starting point.** Humanoid shipments were 13,318 in 2025 ([Omdia via TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/01/09/chinas-agibot-leads-global-humanoid-robot-shipments-in-2025-omdia-says/)) and about 19,000-25,000 in H1 2026 ([IDC, Oct 2](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/10/02/idc-data-shows-agibot-led-global-humanoid-robot-shipments-in-h1-2026/105463/)), over 97% Chinese. The often-quoted 4-5 month doubling is year-over-year, inflated by back-loaded 2025 output. Within 2026 the leaders are linear. AgiBot made about 5,000 a quarter and reached 20,000 cumulative on Sep 24 ([Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/agibot-20000-robots-production-milestone)). UBTech sold 921 full-size units in H1 2026 against 1,079 in all of 2025 ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/ubtechs-full-size-humanoid-robot-revenue-jumps-1445-in-h1-2026)). The IFR says most humanoids "often need human teleoperation" ([IFR, Sep 30](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/asia-press-releases/global-sales-of-professional-service-robots-surge-24-1458551)), and in January Musk said no Optimus did useful work at Tesla ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/01/28/musk-admits-no-optimus-robots-are-doing-useful-work-at-tesla-after-claiming-otherwise/)). Only a few thousand humanoids do paid work.

**Fleet paths** (general-purpose robots, millions, 5-year life). Base starts from 60k shipped in 2026 and grows 2.3x a year in 2027-28, before robots are broadly useful, then 2.4x and 2.2x as usefulness lands. After 2031 the loop starts working in the robot supply chain, so growth eases to 1.8x and then 1.6x, not the 1.2-1.3x of peacetime analogues.

Sensitivities: early onset (the draft's old base) gives 9.0M in 2031 and 71M in 2035. Holding a 12-month doubling after 2031 gives 104M in 2035. Saturating at 1.3x a year gives 30M.

Cross-checks:

- Base 2031 output (3.4M a year) matches the infrastructure research's Epoch-anchored central (3.5M). [Epoch (Apr 2026)](https://epoch.ai/blog/how-fast-could-robot-production-scale-up) calls 1.5-3M a year by end-2030 "clearly achievable" after a demand shock.
- Bank 2030 forecasts of 0.45-1.2M ([Goldman 0.89M](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/14/goldman-sachs-just-supercharged-its-humanoid-robot-prediction-5x-to-6-5-million-by-2035/)) sit between slow and base. Goldman's 6.5M for 2035 is a quarter of base, which in turn is about a quarter of world car output by count (general).
- Even base is historic: a 10-month doubling for five years matches US aircraft in 1940-44, which then went flat ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_aircraft_production_during_World_War_II)). The S4 premise supplies the demand: a $20-30k robot worth about 1.5 workers pays back in under a year.

The S4 premise, that S3 cognition makes dexterity mainly software by about 2028, is an assumption. Field reliability is physical. The forearm was Figure 02's top failure point at BMW ([Figure](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw)), and no maker publishes mean time between interventions. Showing 1,000 hours between interventions at 90% confidence takes about 2,300 failure-free hours per task (general). The EU Machinery Regulation applies from 20 January 2027 ([European Commission](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/mechanical-engineering/machinery_en)) with third-party assessment of machine-learning safety functions (general); ISO 25785-1 is likely around 2028.

## 4. Gate by gate: what robots compress and what they cannot

**The rule.** A robot works 24/7 and in parallel. It removes labor, shift and queue time and turns capacity limits into capital limits. It cannot shorten a step set by physics, statistics, orbital mechanics, biology or statute. S4 counts only general-purpose robots beyond the specialized automation S3 already includes.

**Labor gain over S3** = robot hours ÷ crew hours × productivity relative to people × a parallel-crew factor. At robot-first firms already running several shifts: 1.35 × 0.85 × 1.25 ≈ 1.4x in 2031 (0.9-2.0x) and 1.4 × 1.1 × 1.5 ≈ 2.3x in 2035 (1.6-3.1x). Today robots are 30-50% as productive as people (UBTech) and typically 2-10x slower ([Epoch, Feb 2026](https://epoch.ai/publications/where-autonomy-works-evaluating-robot-capabilities-in-2026)).

**Where robots concentrate, labor often does not bind.** Rocket Lab's Rosie cut Electron composite work from 400+ hours to 12 ([TechCrunch, 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/13/rocket-labs-new-rosie-the-robot-speeds-up-launch-vehicle-production-by-a-lot)); cadence rose 3.5x in six years. SpaceX builds about 70 Starlink satellites a week and keeps Starship hulls ahead of flights ([S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001181412/000162828026040364/spaceexplorationtechnologib.htm)). Launch binds.

**Space gates.** Calendar time as a fraction of the 2026 duration. For a typical program, S4 2031 equals S3 in every row, because robots supply about 3% of aerospace production labor. S3 2035 is taken as 10% below S3 2031.

## 5. Amdahl by domain

**Space hardware program.** Shares: 35% cognitive, fabrication 25%, integration and test 25%, qualification 10%, launch 5%. Each physical slice splits into labor, physics, materials-capital and institutions, calibrated to the model's S3 51%. In every S4 case, physics time constants and institutions stay at S3 values for the same year. Parallel test articles earn credit only on serial build-test-learn iterations, only at robot-first firms, as a capital lever.

| Case | Ceiling: % shorter (speedup) | Realized |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S2 2031 | 38% (1.6x) | 20-30% |
| S3 2031 | 51% (2.05x) | 35-45% |
| S4 2031, typical program (robots on 5-15% of labor steps) | 52% (2.1x) | 35-45% |
| S4 2031, leading firm (robots on half) | 54% (2.2x) | 38-46% |
| S4 2031, robot-first firm | 56-60% (2.3-2.5x) | 40-50% |
| S3 2035 | 56% (2.25x) | 40-48% |
| S4 2035, typical / leading | 58% / 62% (2.4x / 2.6x) | 42-54% |
| S4 2035, robot-first firm | 66-67% (2.9-3.05x) | 50-58% |
| Floor: infinite cognition and robots | 67-70% (2031), 70-73% (2035) | n/a |
| Floor with institutions 1.5x faster (non-robotic) | 75% (4.0x) | n/a |

Robot-first 2031 slice speedups (fabrication 1.5x, I&T 1.75x, qualification 1.6x) match the capability research's independent proposal. The 56% end includes requalification drag.

What remains: at S3 2031, labor is 28% of the remaining critical path and physics 35%. At S4 2035 robot-first, labor is 16%, physics 44%, materials and capital 18%, institutions 20%. Schedules end up set by physics and paperwork.

**Infrastructure, drugs and materials.** "Robots only" credits only lab, field-labor and specimen steps. "With institutions" adds faster reviews, phase overlap and acceptance of model-based allowables, which S3 could equally get.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Permissive or behind-the-meter build | 18% | 18%; 21% | 25% | 27%; 37% | Ceiling 80% |

- **Infrastructure.** Robots supply about 1% of U.S. construction labor by 2031. Best practice is already 3-12x faster than typical without them: xAI built Colossus 1 in 122 days with human crews ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/xais-colossus-2-first-gigawatt-datacenter)). Interconnection takes 61 months median ([LBNL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/queued_up_2026_edition.pdf)), and construction productivity fell for decades ([Goolsbee and Syverson](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30845)).
- **Drugs.** Phase I through review takes 10.5 of 16 years ([BIO](https://www.bio.org/clinical-development-success-rates-and-contributing-factors-2011-2020)). Even infinitely fast labs cap the gain at 34%. Clinical operations can shrink through AI screening ([JAMA RCT](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2830514)), but that is cognitive work. Robots' bigger lever is probability of success: lifting Phase II success from 29% to 40-50% cuts programs per approval by 28-42%.
- **Materials.** Robots collapse synthesis; characterization now binds (A-Lab: 36 of 40 claims confirmed, [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12872444/)). Allowables need lots from separate heats over time, which parallel frames cannot compress.

## 6. The feedback loop: when it bites and what caps it

**Super-exponential before 2035? No.** That would need the doubling time to keep shrinking; every complex-machine ramp has done the opposite once a gate binds. FANUC has built robots with robots since 2001, yet output doubled only about every 7 years because demand set the rate ([FANUC](https://fanuc.co.jp/en/ir/announce_other/pdf/2016/notice20161011_e.pdf)). [Forethought](https://forethought.org/research/the-industrial-explosion) puts self-replicating robots at about a 1-year doubling with abundant AI, "a few years" with real frictions.

**What the loop buys:**

- **Little by 2031.** 3.4M robots a year at 100-300 hours each is 0.2-0.5M worker-equivalents, 3-8% of the 2031 fleet's labor. Figure ramped its line 24x in under 120 days with people. Labor is not what binds, so the loop adds about 1.0-1.2x by 2031.
- **Real gains from about 2032.** That is when robots start working in reducer, screw and motor plants. Holding a 12-month doubling to 2035 would give about 104M robots instead of 62M.
- **The order it bites in:** robot makers' own lines in 2029-30, the robot supply chain in 2031-33, capital goods (machine tools, furnaces, fabs, construction) in the mid-2030s.

**What caps it**

| Cap | When and how it binds |
| --- | --- |
| Precision actuators | Western chains 2026-28. Chinese supply is elastic: Leaderdrive went from 0.43M (2025) to over 1M of capacity (2026) ([Humanoid Guide](https://humanoid.guide/leaderdrive-harmonic-reducers-surge-as-humanoid-demand-lifts-shares/)), an 11-month doubling that matches the base path |
| Reliability | 2026-30: field hours per task; 12-30 month hardware generations |
| NdFeB magnets | China's licensing pause lapses about Nov 10 2026. Magnet mass per robot is a design choice: base 2035 needs 26, 53 or 118 kt at 1, 2 or 4.5 kg per robot (8-36% of 2025 output of ~330 kt); fast needs 69-309 kt ([FAI](https://policygradients.thefai.org/p/the-robot-boom-has-a-magnet-problem)) |
| Capital | Base $50-100B a year in 2031 and $0.4-0.8T in 2035; fast passes $1T a year around 2035 |
| Compute and power (shared with S3) | Robot training and factories compete with AI data centers for chips, turbines and interconnection (Figure is reserving up to 100k GPUs) |
| Politics, labor, safety | EU assessment from 2027; ISO 25785-1 ~2028; unions; state backlash |
| Energy | Not for robots (base fleet ~9 TWh in 2031, ~93 TWh in 2035); yes for compute, fabs and any economy-wide doubling |

**Baumol and weak links.** Growth is capped by the hardest essential task not yet automated ([Aghion, Jones and Jones](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23928); [Jones and Tonetti](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/02/past-automation-and-future-a-i-how-weak-links-tame-the-growth-explosion.html)). So we measure doubling in physical units and name each weak link.

| Physical output | Earliest under-2-year doubling | Band | What drives it | Weak link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General-purpose robots (units) | Already; base holds ~10 months to 2031 | High | Small base, capital | Actuators, reliability |
| Lab experiments per year | Already (workcells) | High | Capex | Characterization |
| Mass to orbit (t) | 2027-28 | Medium | Starship reuse, not robots | Pads, flight failures |
| Satellites (mass per year) | 2027-29 | Medium | Starship V3, orbital compute | Launch |
| Lunar landed mass and robot-hours | 2028-30 | Medium | Tiny base | Landers, power |
| Manufacturing output, leading economy | 2034-35 | Low by 2035 | Robots at ~10% of labor | Energy, equipment |
| Construction, grid electricity, drug approvals, magnets | After 2035 | Speculative | n/a | Permits, turbines, trials, mines |

What doubles fast is small or rides launch's own exponential. Large mature sectors do not, before 2035.

## 7. What changes in the Space Unlock Tree

The draft moved 19 of 150 tier 5-7 nodes earlier. All 19 revert to their S3 year and band. Three broke hard prerequisites (100+ heavy launches before pads; shorter qualification before analysis credit; engine development before the stability tool). Eight landed in 2028, before robots are useful in aerospace. The rest are bound by launch, flights, landed mass or institutions.

**By 2031:**

- 0 nodes move by a year or a band. High-or-Medium stays 92, High stays 35.
- 6 gain about a quarter-year within their year: radiator serial production, AI-designed heat-shield material and alloy flights, engine development cut, shorter qualification campaigns, stage wear tracking.
- 2 more (100+ heavy launches, rapidly reusable heat shield) gain a quarter-year only if Starship ship turnaround falls under a week.
- Mean gain over all 150 is about 0.01 years, against 0.39 years for S3 over S2.

**By 2035, S4 credit appears** for robot-built lunar pads, robotic assembly in space, a self-running lunar base, lunar oxygen at tonne scale, pads and propellant plants, 12-14 km/s entry, kilometre-scale orbital structures and a three-year launcher at a leading firm. Five-year materials still need model-based allowables, an institutional change.

**Node to add:** a robotic-process requalification standard for flight hardware, earliest about 2029-30, Medium. It gates S4 in aerospace.

## 9. Signposts

- **2026 shipments.** Under 50k points to slow, 60-100k to base, over 150k to fast. H1 suggests the slow-base boundary. Watch quarterly output at AgiBot and UBTech break from linear.
- **Useful work.** A maker publishes fleet mean time between interventions; Tesla reports Optimus units in productive use.
